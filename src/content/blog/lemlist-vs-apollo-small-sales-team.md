## The short answer

If your team already knows who it wants to contact, start with lemlist. If the hard part is finding those people in the first place, start with Apollo.

Both can send sequences and both can find contacts. The difference is which half each one was built around.

## What each one is built for

**lemlist** is an outreach tool first. Its strength is multichannel sequences across email and LinkedIn, with personalization and deliverability tools around them.

**Apollo** is a B2B contact database first. It combines an email finder, sequences and a built-in CRM, so a small team can build a list and work it in one place.

## How we compare them

We look at the same five things for every outbound tool we recommend to a client:

1. **Data** Where the contacts come from and how they are kept up to date.
2. **Channels** Email only, or email plus LinkedIn steps in the same sequence.
3. **Deliverability** Warm-up, sending limits and inbox rotation.
4. **CRM sync** Whether replies and meetings land in the CRM without manual work.
5. **Cost per seat** What the team pays once it outgrows the trial.

## When we pick lemlist

We pick lemlist when the list comes from somewhere else: a CRM export, an event, a LinkedIn search the founder already runs. The team then spends its time on the message and the sequence, which is where lemlist is strongest.

## When we pick Apollo

We pick Apollo when the team has no list yet and needs to build one every week. Having the database, the sequence and a light CRM in one tool removes a lot of copy-pasting for a team of five.

## Using both

Some teams build lists in Apollo and run sequences in lemlist. It works, but it is one more sync to maintain. We only recommend it once the team sends enough volume that the stronger sequencing pays for the extra tool.
